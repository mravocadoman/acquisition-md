## 1. The print-on-demand project is confidential

**Never name it. Never reveal the niche, theme, franchise or what the product depicts.** Screenshots, carousel slides and kit pages included. The scheduled-task prompts that run the store name it; the posts and kits derived from them never do.

Refer to it only as:
- "a print-on-demand ecommerce project I run"
- "a print-on-demand side project"
- "an AI product I built"

Vary the phrasing.

**The mechanics are fair game** — and they are the interesting part:

- purchase-first architecture (pay before any compute runs)
- the AI review layer scoring its own output (two axes, 85%, three attempts, auto-refund)
- immutable approval snapshots, HMAC-signed expiring links
- Printify fulfilment, duplicate-push guard, 4x upscale before print
- the 25 documented iterations, four of which were about the AI
- the six scheduled Claude sessions that run its operations (health check, truth report, media buyer, refund reconciler, alert audit, Monday one-pager)

**Caveat:** the Proof Assets block in the generator still names the business and describes the theme. The system message instructs the model to ignore that name. If a draft ever slips the name through, the durable fix is to scrub the Proof Assets Set node itself.

## 2. The audience has never heard of it

Nothing has been announced publicly. Any post touching the project must introduce it in half a sentence on first mention — *"a print-on-demand ecommerce project I run"* — then move on. Never open assuming familiarity.

Same principle applies to any project, client or programme the reader has no reason to know.

## 3. Every post stands alone (29 Sep)

No post may depend on the reader having seen a previous one. No "yesterday I…", no "tomorrow you'll see…", no proper nouns for a series. A light through-line in a first comment ("that one is Tuesday", "that kit is called TEAM and went out on Monday") is fine; a dependency is not.

## 4. Lead magnets lead. Three of four posts a week carry a kit; the fourth is an opinion post (29 Sep, late; mix changed 30 Sep, late, and again 12 Oct)

The audience wants something big and valuable, framed with an impressive claim, delivered free. So the lead magnets are the headline, and since 12 Oct the mix per week is **three lead-magnet posts and one opinion post** (Rihards: "let's mostly focus on lead magnets? With few opinion posts"). A NUMBERS post or a short STORY takes a kit slot only when no kit is ready that week; it is the fallback, not a fixture. The history: three kits a week on 29 Sep; two kits + one opinion + one numbers/story from 30 Sep, because six gated posts in a fortnight read as one template; back to three kits on 12 Oct because the kits are what his audience asks for, with the opinion post kept as the one place comments come from. Batch One (Mon 19 to Fri 30 Oct) is six kits and two opinion posts: TEAM, ADS, CAROUSEL, START, LEADS and REVIEW, plus "An empty Ads Library is not a gap in the market" and the ROAS opinion; the coach story is archived.

A lead-magnet post:
- **opens with the claim**: "My entire marketing department runs on Claude and n8n", "I handed my Meta ads to Claude", "Every carousel on this profile is drawn by 726 lines of Python", "I started an ecommerce business with Claude". Exaggerate the frame, never the fact (the slate's rule). Every clause in the claim must be literally true and checkable against a running system.
- **shows the mechanism** in 4 to 7 arrow lines with real settings and numbers. This is the part people save.
- **has one honest line**: what it costs, what it does not do, how long it has run. This is what makes the claim believable.
- **names the kit and what is inside**, then asks: "Comment KEYWORD and I will send it to you. Free, no email, no form."
- **is 150 to 250 words** (1 Oct; it was 220-360 on 29 Sep). The mechanism list is the value; everything around it is connective tissue, and the tightened Batch One scored the same or higher with Jev at the shorter length. Short paragraphs, one list, no sentence that only restates the one before it.

The kit must exist as a hosted page before the post is written (rule 12). If the material for a new kit exists (a running system, a set of prompts, a log), the writing session builds the kit first, then the post. The ADS kit was built this way on 29 Sep from the live task prompts. Three kits a week means the Sunday session's first job is to find or build the third kit; when it cannot, that slot becomes a NUMBERS post or a STORY, and the session says so in its report rather than padding a thin kit.

The free-value post, when one runs, is there for credibility and rhythm: a NUMBERS post (two of his real figures set next to each other) or a STORY under 260 words with a dry beat and his own photo. Never bulleted for a story.

The **opinion post** (HOT TAKE) is 150 to 230 words: a belief in line one stated as a rule ("An empty Ads Library is not a gap in the market"), the reasoning in three or four short moves with one real number or one real check per move, one line that says where the rule stops, and a question at the end that people can answer from their own experience. No kit, no keyword; the deck ends on the outro question and a save line. The belief must be one he actually holds and has acted on (the Ads Library post comes from a lead-gen idea he researched and did not start). In Notion the Day Type is "Lead Magnet" for a kit post and "Free Value" for an opinion, numbers or story post.

Material that fits none of these gets re-angled or killed. Never padded.

## 5. Hooks are written and scored before the body

8–10 hook options across at least six patterns, each scored 0–3 on specificity, tension, clarity and voice fit. **Nothing scoring below 9 out of 12 gets used.** For a lead-magnet post the hook is the claim, and the claim also becomes the cover headline under the drawn hook, so it has to survive at 58-point type in two or three lines with one word or number worth highlighting.

## 6. The ask: in the text and on the last slide, never a link (29 Sep, late; supersedes both earlier 29 Sep versions)

- Every lead-magnet post ends with **"Comment KEYWORD and I will send it to you. Free, no email, no form."** in the text, and the carousel's last slide carries the same ask in an amber box with the kit URL in small print. That is the pattern Rihards sees working; his call.
- **No URL in the body and no URL in any comment**, ever. External links in the body cost reach and the "link in the first comment" workaround has been penalised since early 2026. The kit URL lives under `=== MAGNET URL ===` in Notion, in the Batch artifact's kit table, and on the slide.

## 7. Numbers must survive their own arithmetic (29 Sep)

Write the sums out in the facts list before the post ships. €0.38 a lead at 2% bought on €1,175 is roughly 8x, a win. 127 replies on 4,837 sent is 2.6%, not 5%. The audience he wants will do the arithmetic in the comments. **The same numbers go on the slides, so if one changes, both change.**

## 8. No story reused within 60 days

Check `content_log`, the calendar and the archive before choosing material. A kit may be re-offered after 60 days with a new frame, not before.

## 9. Voice tells to avoid

Not because they are wrong once, but because the corpus showed them every time: "honest / honestly" as a structural word; "Context:" or "The generalisable bit:" left in the copy; an aphorism couplet as the ending; "What is the best X you ever Y?" as the closing question; four different sign-offs in a week; spaced hyphens used at em-dash frequency. Hyphens only, no em dashes, no double hyphens.

## 11. Accuracy

- No number that isn't in Proof Assets, his own notes, the live task prompts, or freshly searched
- Claims about systems must match what is actually running the day the post goes out ("six scheduled sessions", "seven live workflows plus two paused"). Check the schedule and n8n before approving. The daily true-ROAS report was rewritten on 12 Oct (ledger-based acquisition ROAS with a P&L headline), so the ADS post's "I have not opened Ads Manager since" and any session count are re-checked against the live triggers before Mon 20 Oct
- No inflated scope — lectured on ≠ built; two countries ≠ four continents
- No invented dates, durations or counts. If the source says "recently", write "recently"
- No relative dates in a post, a comment or a slide (12 Oct): "last Monday", "a week in", "that one is coming" go stale the moment a batch slips, and Batch One slipped twice. Write the date ("On 29 September") or a durable phrase ("running since"), and point a through-line at a weekday only when the order of the batch is settled
- First names only for people he knows
- No claims about his health or personal history that he has not confirmed in writing
- A claim only he can make ("I have not opened Ads Manager since") is flagged in the facts list so he can strike it

## 12. Claude never approves, and never posts before approval

n8n keeps a draft and a published version. **Editing a workflow does not change what runs on a trigger.** Every change must be followed by publishing the workflow. (The hosting helpers in rule 10 are the exception: they run manually and are never published.)
